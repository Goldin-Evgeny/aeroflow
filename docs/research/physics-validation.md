# physics-validation

## Summary

The canonical LBM validation ladder is fully specifiable from open literature, in increasing difficulty: (1) 2D Poiseuille — with halfway bounce-back the wall sits midway between nodes but BGK produces a viscosity(τ)-dependent numerical slip; switching to TRT collision with magic parameter Λ=3/16 makes the wall location viscosity-independent and reproduces the parabolic profile exactly (machine precision), so the acceptance test is: BGK error O(Δx²) and TRT(Λ=3/16) error ≈ float round-off. (2) Lid-driven cavity vs Ghia et al. 1982 (257×257 multigrid): key targets Re=100 u_min=−0.21090 @ y=0.4531, u(y=0.5)=−0.20581, v_min=−0.24533 @ x=0.8047; Re=1000 u_min=−0.38289 @ y=0.1719, v_min=−0.51500 @ x=0.9063, v_max=0.37095 @ x=0.1563 (full 17-point tables retrieved). (3) Cylinder shedding: Re=100 St=0.164–0.166 (Tritton exp. 0.164), Cd=1.33–1.39; Re=200 (2D) Cd=1.29–1.41 (Braza 1.40±0.05, Liu 1.31±0.049), St≈0.195–0.197 — with the caveat that real flow goes 3D (mode-A) at Re≈190 so 2D codes should be judged against 2D consensus. (4) Sphere drag: Cd≈1.09 (Re=100, Schiller–Naumann 1.092), ≈0.46–0.47 (Re=1000), ≈0.40–0.42 (Re=10⁴; Newton-regime envelope 0.38@5×10³ to 0.50@7×10⁴); Re=10⁴ requires LES + momentum-exchange forces and is the first "hard" 3D milestone. (5) Ahmed body 25° slant: exp Cd=0.285–0.2875; this is the classic failure case for RANS; LBM path is Fares 2006 (PowerFLOW 4.0, D3Q19 VLES + wall functions, embedded refinement, targeting the industry ±5% band) — reference-quality finite-volume k-ω SST gets 1.2–3.7% error at 3.7–10.1M cells, so an LBM-LES code hitting ±10% with ~50–150M cells (feasible in 8GB VRAM at 55 B/cell) is a defensible target. (6) Urban wedge: AIJ benchmark suite (Tominaga et al. 2008, JWEIA 96:1749–1761; 7 cases): Case A isolated 1:1:2 building (Meng & Hibi wind tunnel), Case C 9-building block, Case D 100 m tower in city blocks (1/400 WT), Case E Niigata neighborhood (1/250 scale, 80 points at 2 m full-scale height, power-law α=0.25 inflow), Case F Shinjuku with field data; AIJ sets no rigid pass/fail — the de-facto quantitative criterion is the VDI 3783/9 hit rate q≥0.66 (allowed deviation 0.25), and published "good" RANS results on Case E score Pearson r=0.71–0.84 with mean bias down to −35%, i.e. the accuracy bar for a screening tool is genuinely low. A 2025 AIJ LES-specific guideline with validation benchmarks now exists — directly relevant since LBM is LES-native.

Solver-parameter facts: LBM-LES Smagorinsky constant Cs≈0.1 (Hou et al. 1996), implemented as a local effective relaxation time τ_eff=τ₀+τ_t with τ_t=½(√(τ₀²+ (const)·Cs²·|Π^neq|) − τ₀) computed per-cell from the non-equilibrium second moment — no gradients or extra storage, ideal for WebGPU. Stability: c_s=1/√3≈0.577, ν=c_s²(τ−½) forces τ>0.5; D2Q9-BGK linear-stability Mach ceiling ≈0.73 but practical lattice velocity must stay ≤0.1–0.2 (compressibility error O(Ma²)); LES-Smagorinsky itself regularizes τ→0.5. Memory: D3Q19 with in-place esoteric-pull streaming = 93 B/cell (FP32) or 55 B/cell (FP32 arithmetic/FP16 storage) vs 169 B/cell two-copy — 8GB VRAM ≈145M cells; FluidX3D shows the arithmetic is bandwidth-bound (RTX 4090 ≈11,500 MLUPs/s D3Q19). Forces: momentum-exchange method (Ladd 1994; key reference Mei, Yu, Shyy & Luo, Phys. Rev. E 65:041203, 2002) is the accepted, cheap, 3D-friendly way to get drag/lift on bounce-back boundaries (stress integration explicitly discouraged); Galilean-invariant corrections (Wen et al.) needed for moving bodies. Rotating turbines: blade-resolved moving boundaries in LBM (interpolated bounce-back + fresh-node refill, or IBM) are the hardest and most resolution-hungry option; actuator-line-in-LBM is proven (Asmuth et al. 2020, cumulant LBM, D/32 grid, <3% wake-deficit error vs EllipSys3D, near-real-time on one GPU; a 2024 2-D ALM-LBM paper exists specifically for VAWTs), while QBlade's DMS/lifting-line is the standard fast VAWT design path (validated, though it overpredicts Cp at high tip-speed ratios). Smart sequence for AeroFlow: DMS/QBlade-style rotor model fed by LBM-computed local wind statistics first, ALM embedded in the LBM later, blade-resolved never (or only as a premium offline feature).
